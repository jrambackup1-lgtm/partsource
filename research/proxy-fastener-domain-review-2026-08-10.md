## Conservative candidate semantics

### 1. Identifier namespace and mapping

**Fundamental requirement — An identifier is a scoped lookup claim, never configuration identity.** Represent at least: `namespace_owner`, `namespace_id`, exact supplied value, namespace-specific normalized value, mapping type, target configuration/revision, evidence record, mapping revision, applicable catalog release, lifecycle state, effective/observed dates where relevant, and review state. A supplier catalog number, manufacturer part number, standard designation, source row key, and PartSource configuration ID are different namespaces even when strings match.

**Fundamental requirement — Exact means exact only inside a declared namespace and release.** Resolve to `one` only when exactly one active, reviewed mapping in the requested release survives. Missing namespace with multiple plausible namespaces, duplicate same-namespace mappings, conflicting targets, outside-release mappings, withdrawn mappings, stale/unavailable release state, or unreviewed remaps must abstain. Never use `LIMIT 1`, first-row order, or a target digest as evidence of uniqueness.

**Fundamental requirement — Mapping identity and engineering selection remain independent axes.** One identifier may identify one source/manufacturer record while critical selection facts remain missing, conflicting, unreviewed, or application-dependent. The safe state is “identified record; selection blocked,” not “unique fastener selected.” A mapping must not imply equivalent, replacement, stocked item, supplier listing, or approved alternate.

**Strong hypothesis — Namespace-specific conservative normalization can improve lookup without changing identity.** Preserve raw input. Permit only reviewed operations for that namespace, such as outer whitespace handling or documented case behavior. Do not globally remove punctuation, leading zeroes, separators, or prefixes; those can be significant. Record normalization rule/version and expose ambiguity rather than broadening silently.

### 2. Metric and unified-inch thread designation

**Fundamental requirement — Store a typed thread ledger, not one display string.** Candidate fields should include measurement system; supplied designation; thread form/profile; nominal diameter as exact decimal or rational plus unit; pitch as exact length per turn for metric; TPI as an independent exact count per inch for unified inch; thread series; direction; external/internal role; tolerance class; intended mating/fit statement if actually supported; thread extent; governing authority/standard/edition; supplied-versus-derived state; derivation rule/version; and conflicts/missingness.

**Fundamental requirement — Pitch and TPI are not interchangeable fields.** S18/S22 distinguish unified threads-per-inch from metric linear pitch, and S18 labels any metric-to-TPI comparison as approximate. Conversion may be an explicitly derived display aid; it must not mutate the canonical quantity, validate a thread plan by rounded equality, or let substring containment count as parsing.

**Fundamental requirement — Nominal diameter is not measured finished diameter.** `M8` is a designation containing a nominal diameter under the M profile, not evidence that every physical major-diameter observation equals the display numeral. Keep nominal/basic/measured dimensions and tolerances separate (S01–S03, S14).

**Strong hypothesis — A pitch omitted from an M designation may be represented as an explicit profile-derived coarse-pitch implication, never as a supplied fact.** S18 describes the common callout convention, and S01 ties permitted diameter/pitch combinations to the ISO plan. The derived value needs a reviewed governing profile/edition, compatible diameter, rule version, and visible `derived` status. If any of those are absent, pitch stays unknown. “Metric” alone never supplies M-profile, diameter, or pitch.

**Fundamental requirement — Unified-inch parsing must retain diameter, TPI, form/series, class, and direction separately.** A string such as `1/4-20` is useful partial evidence, not a complete universal thread specification. `UNC`, `UNF`, `UNR`, `UNJ`, and special series/forms must not be merged because a diameter/TPI pair happens to match. S13 expressly scopes form, series, class, allowance, tolerance, and designation.

**Fundamental requirement — Direction and tolerance/fit cannot be silently defaulted at catalog-truth level.** Right-hand may be a convention under a specific governing designation, but a parser must retain whether direction was supplied, lawfully/profile-derived, or unknown. Left-hand products exist in official fastener catalogs. Likewise, an external tolerance class, internal tolerance class, and claimed mating fit are related but distinct; absence is not conformance.

### 3. Length and thread extent

**Fundamental requirement — Length is a typed quantity plus a product-specific basis.** Store exact value/unit, supplied text, and basis such as `under_head_bearing_surface`, `overall_including_countersunk_head`, `shoulder_length`, or another approved profile datum. Store threaded length/extent, grip/body length, and shoulder length separately. S04/S05 make nominal length, thread length, symbols, and definitions separate concerns; S18/S24 demonstrate head-form-dependent manufacturer treatment.

**Risk — Comparing bare lengths across head forms creates false matches.** `20 mm` on a pan/standard socket screw and `20 mm` on a countersunk, shoulder, set, or captive-washer product cannot be assumed to describe the same physical span. Frozen v1 stores only supplied length text and no length basis.

### 4. Head, drive, point, and assembly geometry

**Fundamental requirement — Model physical recess geometry and query alternatives separately.** A physical `slotted_phillips_combination` recess may expose both features (S23). In contrast, a user saying “Phillips or slotted” may express alternatives. Use a controlled combined-drive value or feature set plus composition semantics; do not mark conflict merely because a list contains two tokens.

**Fundamental requirement — Family profiles require positive geometry and negative neighbors.** A profile should state required head shape/profile, drive/recess, bearing/collar/flange state, point/end, captive washer/SEMS state, thread type, and loadability boundary where relevant. Test excluded neighbors explicitly rather than treating an import bucket as a family.

**Strong hypothesis — Initial socket-family boundaries can be conservative.** A standard-profile hexagon socket head cap screw profile can reference S06 and exclude low/high/ultra-low profiles, button heads, button heads with collar, countersunk/flat heads, shoulder screws, set screws, pilot-recess variants, flange/collar forms, captive-washer assemblies, hexalobular drives, and tapping/drilling forms. S07/S08/S15/S18/S20/S21 show that these are meaningful neighboring forms. This is a candidate profile for qualified review, not an approved taxonomy.

**Open question — Pan-head parentage remains unresolved.** Phillips, slotted, combination, and hexalobular recesses may be drive facets of one bounded machine-screw head family, while tapping/drilling threads and captive-washer assemblies may require separate families. The repository cannot decide that taxonomy from text or import buckets. Until reviewed, retain explicit candidates and exclusions rather than forcing one family.

### 5. Material, finish/coating, and strength

**Fundamental requirement — Preserve and separate five layers:** supplied phrase; base material family; alloy/grade/designation and condition/heat treatment; finish/coating system; and mechanical strength/property data. Keep property class, grade, proof/tensile/yield values, hardness scale/value, and reduced-loadability limitations as different claims with their own source and applicability.

**Evidence — Primary sources support the separation.** ISO 898-1 and ISO 3506-1 have different material/property scopes (S09/S10); coating has its own requirements and vocabulary (S11/S12); and Unbrako separately lists material, hardness, tensile strength, and yield strength (S18). Bossard separately warns that low-head geometry changes loadability (S20). Therefore a single coalesced `strength` string and a finish-bearing `material` string cannot safely drive equality, range comparisons, or substitution.

**Fundamental requirement — Conflict requires a typed exclusivity rule.** Two supplied phrases can be aliases, hierarchy members, coating-plus-substrate composition, alternatives, or genuine contradictions. Preserve both and their source spans. Declare conflict only after controlled taxonomy and composition rules establish mutual exclusivity. Never use list length alone.

### 6. Standards, editions, and relationships

**Fundamental requirement — Standard references need issuer, identifier, part, edition/date, lifecycle, and relationship type.** Relationship types should distinguish `claimed_conformance`, `dimensions_from`, `mechanical_properties_from`, `similar_to`, `supersedes`, `withdrawn`, and unresolved supplied text. A bare `DIN 7984`, `ISO 4762`, or `B18.3` string is not a complete configuration or cross-standard equivalence.

**Evidence — Parts and lifecycle materially change meaning.** ISO 7380-1 and -2 distinguish plain button and collar forms (S07/S08), while ISO 965-1 has a 2026 edition (S03). ASME B18.3 covers multiple socket-product types (S15). Manufacturer wording such as “similar specification” is not identity or conformance. Standards also define configurations that need not be manufactured or stocked; product existence and commercial state stay separate under the product contract.

### 7. Conflict, missingness, and safe response states

**Fundamental requirement — Use at least four fact states:** supplied/known, unknown/missing, not applicable under an approved profile, and explicit conflict. Add unsupported scope and unreviewed derivation independently. Blank, dash, absent column, and “not applicable” are not automatically the same.

**Fundamental requirement — Candidate labels must describe what was actually checked.** If typed constraints were not evaluated against records, return broad/unvalidated candidates with preserved constraints and a warning; do not call them `constrained_candidates`. If every displayed record is checked, expose per-fact `satisfies`, `contradicts`, `missing`, or `not_applicable` status. Any critical contradiction or unknown blocks unique selection.

**Fundamental requirement — Criticality is operation-specific and profile-versioned.** Exact identifier routing may need namespace, release, lifecycle, and mapping review even when mechanical fields are missing. A selectable configuration needs the approved family-required fields and every user-supplied hard constraint. A sourcing handoff may preserve unresolved facts but must not imply suitability, equivalence, listing, stock, price, or availability.

**Opportunity / gold idea — Make the constraint ledger the review and test oracle.** For each supplied span, show raw value, parsed type/quantity, normalized or derived value, unit, authority/profile/rule revision, candidate comparison state, conflict/missingness, and reviewer disposition. A differences-first packet can simultaneously expose dropped facts, unsafe defaults, family exclusions, source conflicts, identifier lifecycle, and handoff preservation without generating prose as truth.

### Risk

- The word `candidate` may be read as “constraint-satisfying” even when no constraint was checked.
- Defaults for coarse pitch, right-hand direction, tolerance class, or length basis can silently become catalog facts.
- Namespace collisions, release drift, remaps, and withdrawn records can produce false exact identity.
- Family leakage and fused material/finish/strength can create false matches that look technically precise.
- More synthetic cases can increase confidence without adding independent truth, human usefulness, or qualified authority.

- Frozen v1 as mechanical accuracy, search quality, release acceptance, family approval, positive unique selection, or production-readiness evidence.

- Automatically inferred equivalence, substitution, compatibility, suitability, supplier listing, stock, price, availability, certification, or organizational approval.
