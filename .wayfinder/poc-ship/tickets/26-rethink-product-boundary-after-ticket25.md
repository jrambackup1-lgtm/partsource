## Question

Which Ticket 25 failures are architectural, data-model, or POC implementation failures; is safe handoff still viable; must the product boundary change; and is there a simpler safer wedge?

## Resolution

**Reject the current automatic safe-handoff direction.**

The 11/11 adversarial failure is not one bad implementation episode.

- Local bugs exist and are repairable.
- Authoritative selection creates a release-authority, identity, lifecycle, and fact-ledger system.
- The current data still lacks approved families, required-field profiles, complete projection, reviewed mapping identity, and qualified release authority.
- Zero direct users and zero qualified reviewers justify none of that machinery.

A human-owned raw-preserving issue list remains technically viable. It is a safety-bounded artifact, not a validated product.

Safe handoff is rejected as the value proposition. The buyer outcome must be fewer clarification loops, corrected lines, or a clearer accepted handoff.

## Alternatives

1. **Recommended test:** paid manual BOM ambiguity audit. No software.
2. Internal catalog data-quality anomaly report for one sanctioned catalog owner.
3. Kill standalone PartSource and reuse the expertise in engineering services.
4. Keep exact-ID explanation only as a possible acquisition hook.
5. Defer automated lint until repeated paid manual use exists.
6. Reject family-first public catalog and supplier-query handoff as lead wedges.

## Recommendation

Historical recommendation: open one HITL decision for a fixed-price no-build BOM ambiguity-audit test.
