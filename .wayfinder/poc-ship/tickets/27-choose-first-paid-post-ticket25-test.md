## Choices

### A — paid manual BOM ambiguity audit — historical proposal, not selected

Test one fixed-price, no-build service on safely redacted 10–50-line fastener BOM excerpts.

Deliver only:

- preserved original lines;
- literal contradictions and duplicate/opaque identifiers;
- parse failures, source sentinels, and unclear wording;
- prioritized questions for engineering or sourcing.

Do not deliver:

- automatic corrected configurations;
- family, standard, material, pitch, strength, finish, or suitability claims without named qualified review;
- BOM-ready, procurement-ready, safe, verified, or approved states;
- equivalents, alternates, supplier matches, price, stock, or availability.

Starting price hypothesis: US$250.

### B — internal catalog QA anomaly report

Test a manual anomaly report with one catalog owner that supplies a sanctioned export and budget.

This is a different B2B direction. It has higher buyer value potential and much heavier access, integration, and enterprise-sales risk.

### C — kill standalone PartSource

Stop product work. Preserve reusable research and code. Route any real paid demand into engineering services instead of keeping an unfunded software shell alive.

### D — revise

Change the offer, buyer, safety boundary, price, or gate before any external action.

## Recommended gate for A

From 12 qualified offers in a fixed window:

1. at least 3 prospects provide a useful recent redacted packet;
2. at least 2 pay or place a nontrivial deposit;
3. at least 2 report the job more than once in the previous 90 days;
4. at least 2 issue lists cause a concrete next action;
5. at least 1 buyer names a comparable future purchase event.

Kill standalone PartSource if no paid pilot results, the artifact changes no action, normal work is faster, or buyers mainly want excluded commercial/procurement outcomes.
